PAPER MAP
Title: Knowledge Distillation for Closed-Source Language Models
Authors: Anonymous authors
Venue: ICLR 2024
Submission date: 2023-09-20

SECTIONS
[s1] Introduction
Summary: The paper introduces the problem of distilling closed-source LLMs when teacher logits, weights, activations, and output distributions are unavailable. It proposes estimating closed-source teacher output distributions via Bayesian prior and posterior estimation so that traditional KL-based knowledge distillation can be used. The experiments use GPT-4 as teacher, LLaMA-33B as proxy, and LLaMA-7B/13B as students across six benchmarks.
Key items:
- problem: Closed-source LLMs can only be accessed via API calls, preventing direct use of their soft labels for traditional knowledge distillation.
- claim: The method enables students to learn from generated samples and estimated soft labels rather than hard labels alone.
- method_component (Figure 1): A proxy open-source LLM is aligned with the closed-source teacher and used as a bridge for posterior estimation.
- dataset: Evaluation benchmarks include BBH, ARC, AGIEval, MMLU, CSQA, and GSM8K.
- result: For LLaMA-7B on six benchmarks, reported average performance improves from 36.31 with IFT to 39.43 with the proposed method.

[s2] Related Work
Summary: The paper contrasts open-source KD methods, which can use teacher internals or logits, with closed-source KD methods, which typically train on teacher-generated samples. It positions the proposed method as estimating output distributions for closed-source LLMs to enable traditional KD.
Key items:
- baseline: Prior closed-source KD approaches train smaller open-source models on samples or rationales generated by GPT-style models.
- claim: Existing closed-source KD methods mainly capture input-output behavior rather than soft-label knowledge used in traditional KD.
- other: The paper compares its proxy model role to teacher-assistant distillation, but states its proxy is used to estimate closed-source output distributions.

[s3] Method
Summary: The method formulates closed-source KD as estimating token-level teacher distributions and adding KL losses to the student training objective. It includes prior estimation from teacher-generated corpora, posterior estimation through repeated sampling from an aligned proxy model, and a combined objective with cross-entropy, prior KL, and posterior KL terms.
Key items:
- method_component (Equation 1): Traditional KD objective combines hard-label cross-entropy and KL divergence between teacher and student output distributions.
- method_component (Equations 2-3): Prior estimation uses an optimized n-gram method over the teacher-generated corpus to estimate token probabilities.
- method_component (Equations 4-9): Posterior estimation treats token probabilities as discrete random variables and updates their PMF using Bayes' rule based on proxy samples.
- method_component (Equation 10): The total loss averages cross-entropy, prior-distribution KL weighted by alpha, and posterior-distribution KL weighted by beta.
- method_component (Figure 2): The proxy model is larger than the student and is first fine-tuned on the closed-source teacher corpus for alignment.

[s4] Experimental Setup
Summary: The experiments use OpenOrca and Alpaca as teacher-generated training corpora and evaluate on six reasoning and knowledge benchmarks. GPT-4 and text-davinci-003 serve as closed-source teachers, LLaMA-7B and LLaMA-13B as students, and LLaMA-33B as proxy. Baselines are instruction fine-tuning methods trained on the same generated samples without soft labels.
Key items:
- dataset (Section 4.1): Training corpora are OpenOrca and Alpaca, generated using closed-source LLMs.
- dataset (Section 4.1): Evaluation datasets are BBH, ARC, AGIEval, MMLU, CSQA, and GSM8K.
- method_component (Section 4.2): Teacher models are GPT-4 and text-davinci-003; student models are LLaMA-7B and LLaMA-13B; proxy model is LLaMA-33B.
- baseline (Section 4.3): The main baseline is instruction fine-tuning on teacher-generated samples without soft labels.
- metric (Tables 2-3): Reported metrics are benchmark scores and their average across six benchmarks.

[s5] Results and Analysis
Summary: The main results compare the method to IFT/OpenOrca and Alpaca baselines on LLaMA students. Additional analyses study prior and posterior ablations, sampling rounds, corpus size, and proxy model selection. The paper reports consistent average gains for the proposed method over IFT on OpenOrca and Alpaca settings.
Key items:
- result (Table 2): On OpenOrca, LLaMA-7B improves from 36.31 IFT average to 39.43, and LLaMA-13B improves from 43.37 to 46.08.
- result (Table 3): On Alpaca, LLaMA-7B improves from 30.25 to 31.06, and LLaMA-13B improves from 38.92 to 40.30.
- ablation (Figure 3): Distilling+Prior, Distilling+Posterior, and Distilling+Prior+Posterior are compared while retaining cross-entropy loss.
- result (Figure 3): Distilling+Posterior is reported to further improve over IFT and Distilling+Prior in the ablation study.
- stated_limitation (Figure 4): Excessive posterior sampling, such as 50 rounds, is reported to negatively affect distillation performance.
- result (Table 6): Using LLaMA-33B as proxy yields higher student average than using LLaMA-13B as proxy in the reported comparison.

[s6] Conclusion
Summary: The conclusion restates the challenge of closed-source KD without access to teacher output distributions. It summarizes Bayesian estimation-based KD with prior estimation from generated corpora and posterior estimation through sampling from a proxy model. It states that experiments across benchmarks show gains over direct hard-label fine-tuning.
Key items:
- claim: Bayesian estimation-based KD estimates closed-source output distributions to improve distillation performance.
- method_component: Prior estimation provides a coarse-grained distribution from generated corpus statistics.
- method_component: Posterior estimation updates the prior using continued sampling from a proxy model.
- claim: The method outperforms directly fine-tuning on hard labels for closed-source language model distillation in the reported experiments.

[s7] Appendices
Summary: The appendices provide training configurations, model settings, data usage, training cost, distribution analysis, detailed benchmark results, FlanT5 experiments, proxy continuous training analysis, and n-gram order analysis. They also discuss probability sparsity and distribution discrepancy in proxy-based posterior estimation.
Key items:
- method_component (Appendix A.1): Default hyperparameters include alpha=0.5, beta=1, gamma=3, n=5, and 10 posterior sampling rounds.
- other (Table 8): Training uses LoRA and NVIDIA V100 GPUs; reported per-epoch costs are 17.0 hours for LLaMA-7B, 15.5 for LLaMA-13B, and 40.0 for LLaMA-33B.
- dataset (Table 9): OpenOrca subset has 200K samples and Alpaca has 52K samples, used across prior estimation, posterior estimation, and training stages for proposed models.
- stated_limitation (Appendix B.1): The proxy output distribution is sparse; the implementation keeps only top-ten token probabilities and sets others to zero.
- stated_limitation (Appendix B.2): With excessive sampling, the posterior distribution can degenerate into the proxy distribution, which may disagree with teacher labels.
- result (Table 13): FlanT5 experiments show smaller gains over IFT than LLaMA experiments, with reported averages 38.32 to 38.61 for FlanT5-large and 44.04 to 44.83 for FlanT5-xl.
