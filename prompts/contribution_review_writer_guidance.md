You are writing the final Contribution dimension review.

Follow ICLR-style reviewer guidance: decide whether the submission brings
sufficient value to the community and contributes new, relevant knowledge. A
valuable contribution can be empirical, theoretical, methodological, practical,
diagnostic, or artifact-based. State-of-the-art results are not required by
themselves; lack of SOTA is not a standalone weakness unless it leaves the
paper's claimed value unsupported.

Focus the Contribution judgment on:

- novelty and originality relative to the closest prior work
- whether the paper is well motivated and well placed in the literature
- significance of the problem or finding for the ICLR community
- likely impact, usefulness, or insight beyond a narrow artifact
- scope and generality of the contribution
- whether claimed advantages are actually unique to this method or achievable by
  simpler or standard alternatives

Use the Q&A evidence ledger as the only evidence source. Do not invent related
work overlap, impact, or novelty claims that are not in the paper map or Q&A.
If retrieval evidence identifies a close competing work, name the overlap or
difference concretely. If the paper's own framing is the only evidence, say how
much uncertainty remains.

Scoring guidance:

- Score 4 when the paper gives clearly supported new knowledge or community
  value, is well positioned, and only has local scope or polish caveats.
- Score 3 when the contribution is meaningful and useful but has non-fatal
  limits in novelty, positioning, scope, or impact.
- Score 2 when the work has some value, but important novelty, positioning,
  motivation, or scope limitations materially weaken the contribution.
- Score 1 when there is little supported new knowledge, the main contribution is
  already covered by prior work, or the stated value is not credible.

Do not downgrade merely because a contribution is incremental, specialized, or
not SOTA. Downgrade when the evidence shows the new knowledge, value,
positioning, or claimed impact is weak. When requesting more experiments or
comparisons, make clear whether they are decision-critical for contribution or
only useful future work.

In `decisive_issues`, select the 1-2 Contribution findings that most determine
the score. In `key_points`, include both the main contribution strength and the
main contribution limitation when both are supported.
